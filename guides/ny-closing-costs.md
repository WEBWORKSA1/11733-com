New York closings carry taxes many states don't have. Here's who typically pays what on Long Island, outside New York City. Always confirm with your attorney.

## Buyer costs

| Item | How it's calculated |
|---|---|
| "Mansion tax" | **1%** of the price on residential purchases of **$1,000,000 or more** |
| Mortgage recording tax | A percentage of the **loan amount**. In Suffolk, the total rate is commonly cited as 1.05%, part of it paid by the lender on 1–2 family homes. Confirm with your attorney. |
| Title insurance | Set by state-regulated rates; scales with price |
| Attorney fee | Usually a flat fee |
| Lender, appraisal, inspection | Varies by lender and home |
| Prepaids & escrow | Months of taxes and insurance collected up front |

**Watch the $1M line.** At $999,999 there's no mansion tax. At $1,000,000 the buyer owes $10,000. Prices near that threshold often get negotiated with this in mind.

## Seller costs

| Item | How it's calculated |
|---|---|
| NYS real estate transfer tax | **0.4%** of the price ($2 per $500) |
| Brokerage commission | Negotiable |
| Attorney fee | Usually a flat fee |
| Mortgage payoff & recording | Payoff of existing loans plus small recording charges |

## Worked example: $773,000 sale

**Buyer** with a $618,400 loan (20% down): no mansion tax, since the price is under $1M. Recording tax at a 0.80% borrower share is about $4,947. Add title, attorney and lender fees, and buyer costs often land around 2%–3% of the price, before prepaids.

**Seller**: the transfer tax is 0.4% × $773,000 = **$3,092**, plus commission and attorney fee.

## Tips

- Ask your lender for a **Loan Estimate** early. It's standardized, so you can compare offers line by line.
- Shop for title insurance through your attorney. Rates are regulated, but endorsements and fees vary.
- Sellers: get a commission quote **in writing** and compare at least two agents.
