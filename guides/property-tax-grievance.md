Your property tax bill equals your **assessed value** multiplied by the tax rates of every taxing district you're in: school, town, county, fire, library and others. You can't grieve the rates. You **can** challenge the assessment if it's higher than it should be. New York calls this a "grievance".

## Step 1: Find your assessment and the equalization rate

Your assessment is on the Town of Brookhaven's assessment roll and your tax bill. Brookhaven, like many New York towns, assesses at a fraction of market value. Divide your assessed value by the town's published **equalization rate** to get the market value the town is implying for your home.

> Implied market value = Assessed value ÷ Equalization rate

If that implied value is clearly **higher** than what your home would sell for, you may be over-assessed.

## Step 2: Gather evidence

The strongest evidence is recent **sales of comparable homes**: similar size, age, condition and location, sold close to the taxable status date. Helpful extras:

- A recent appraisal
- Photos of defects (a failing roof, water damage, an old cesspool)
- Contractor estimates for needed repairs
- Errors on your property record, such as wrong square footage or a bathroom count

Our free [home value report](/home-value.html) includes comparable sales you can use as a starting point.

## Step 3: File on time

In New York, grievances go to the town's **Board of Assessment Review** using state form **RP-524**. Most towns hold Grievance Day on the **third Tuesday of May**. Confirm the exact Brookhaven deadline with the assessor each year, because late filings are rejected.

## Step 4: If you lose, you have options

If the Board of Assessment Review denies your grievance, you can file for **Small Claims Assessment Review (SCAR)** for an owner-occupied one-, two- or three-family home, or bring a tax certiorari proceeding. SCAR has a modest filing fee and doesn't require a lawyer.

## Should you hire a grievance firm?

Many Long Island firms file grievances on contingency, usually taking a share of the first year's savings and nothing if there's no reduction. It's convenient, but you can file yourself for free. Read any contract carefully, especially multi-year terms.

## What it's worth

At an effective rate around 2%, every **$50,000** of over-assessment in market value costs roughly **$1,000 a year**. That adds up to $10,000 over a decade. See your own numbers in the [property tax estimator](/property-tax.html).
