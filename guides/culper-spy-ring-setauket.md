Long before 11733 was a ZIP code, Setauket was the base of one of the most consequential intelligence operations in American history.

## Why Washington needed spies

After the British captured New York City in 1776, and after the capture and execution of Nathan Hale, George Washington needed a reliable way to learn what the British were planning in Manhattan. In 1778, Major **Benjamin Tallmadge**, who grew up in Setauket, organized a network of trusted people from his hometown.

## The ring

The ring's local leader was **Abraham Woodhull**, a Setauket farmer who wrote as **"Samuel Culper"** (later "Samuel Culper Sr."). In Manhattan, merchant **Robert Townsend**, known as **"Samuel Culper Jr."**, gathered information. Couriers such as **Austin Roe** carried messages across Long Island, and whaleboat captain **Caleb Brewster** ferried them across Long Island Sound to Connecticut. Local tradition holds that **Anna Smith Strong** signaled Brewster's location using laundry hung on her clothesline. Historians still debate how much of that story is documented.

## Tradecraft

The Culper agents used a numbered code dictionary, aliases and an invisible ink, sometimes called a "sympathetic stain", supplied through Washington. Tallmadge himself appeared in the codes as "John Bolton". Townsend's identity as Culper Jr. stayed hidden until historian Morton Pennypacker matched his handwriting in the 1930s.

## Separating fact from fiction

The AMC series *TURN: Washington's Spies* (2014–2017) brought the ring global attention, but it took dramatic liberties. Local historians, including the Three Village Historical Society and author Bill Bleyer, have worked to separate documented history from legend.

## See it for yourself

- **Three Village Historical Society**: the "SPIES!" exhibit and the annual **Culper Spy Day**, a collaboration of dozens of historical and cultural groups
- **Setauket Village Green** and its historic churches
- **Stony Brook University Libraries Special Collections**, which acquired Revolutionary War letters from Washington to Tallmadge about the ring's operations
- Guided walking, biking and kayaking tours that follow the spies' routes
