Buying in 11733 means competing for well-kept, owner-occupied homes near a major university and medical center. Preparation wins. Here's the order of operations.

## 1. Get fully pre-approved

A pre-qualification is a quick guess. A pre-approval means a lender has reviewed your income, assets and credit. Sellers here often see multiple offers, and the strongest ones come with a full pre-approval and proof of funds for the down payment.

Rates move every week. Freddie Mac's survey had the 30-year fixed at **7.28%** on November 19, 2026. Comparing two or three lenders can easily save a quarter point. [Get matched with lenders →](/buyers.html)

## 2. Budget the whole payment, not just the mortgage

On Long Island, property tax is often the second-biggest line in a housing budget. Published estimates put Suffolk County's effective rate at roughly **1.85%–2.1%** of market value, and individual school districts vary. At those rates, a $773K home carries about **$1,190–$1,350 a month** in property tax alone.

Use the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage.html) for full PITI and the [affordability tool](/affordability.html) to check your limit.

## 3. Choose your team

- **Buyer's agent**: since the 2024 industry settlement, you'll sign a written buyer agreement that spells out how your agent is paid. Read it.
- **Real estate attorney**: in New York, attorneys usually negotiate the contract after your offer is accepted. Your offer isn't binding until the contract is signed.
- **Home inspector**: choose someone who knows older Long Island homes.

## 4. Inspect beyond the walls

Ask your inspector and attorney about:

- **Cesspool or septic**: age, last pump-out and condition. Many Suffolk homes aren't on sewers.
- **Underground oil tanks**: abandoned tanks can be costly to remove.
- **Permits and certificates of occupancy** for decks, finished basements and additions.
- **Flood zone status**: check the FEMA flood map, especially near the harbors. See our [flood and insurance guide](/guides/flood-insurance-north-shore.html).

## 5. Make a clean, strong offer

Price matters, but so do terms: a larger down payment, a flexible closing date and a short attorney-review window all help. Avoid waiving inspections. Instead, consider an "informational" inspection that you won't use to renegotiate small items.

## 6. Plan your cash to close

New York buyers pay some costs that surprise newcomers:

- The **1% "mansion tax"** on residential purchases of **$1 million or more**
- **Mortgage recording tax** on the loan amount
- Title insurance, attorney, appraisal and lender fees

Estimate all of it with the [closing cost calculator](/closing-costs.html).

## 7. After closing: claim your exemptions

New homeowners can register for New York's **STAR** credit, which lowers school taxes for eligible owners (income limits apply). Check other exemptions with the Town of Brookhaven assessor too.
