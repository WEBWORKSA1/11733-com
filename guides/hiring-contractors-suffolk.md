Long Island homes are expensive to maintain: older roofs, cesspools, heating systems and storm damage add up. The right contractor saves you money. The wrong one can cost you the job twice. Here's how to hire well in Suffolk County.

## 1. Check the license

Suffolk County requires most **home improvement contractors** to hold a county license issued through its consumer affairs office. Some trades, such as plumbers and electricians, have separate licensing. Before you sign anything:

- Ask for the license number and look it up with the county
- Confirm the business name on the license matches the contract
- Ask whether any complaints have been filed

## 2. Ask for insurance certificates

Request certificates of **general liability** and **workers' compensation** insurance sent directly from the insurer, with you named as certificate holder. If an uninsured worker is hurt on your property, you could face the claim.

## 3. Get three written quotes

Compare scope, not just price. A good quote lists materials (brand and model), quantities, who pulls permits, the timeline, cleanup and the warranty. A quote far below the others usually means something is missing. [Get up to 3 screened quotes →](/get-quotes.html)

## 4. Insist on a written contract

New York law requires written contracts for home improvement work above a modest dollar amount. A solid contract includes:

- Start and completion dates
- A detailed description of the work and materials
- The total price and a **payment schedule tied to milestones**
- Your right to cancel within **three business days** for contracts signed at home
- Who obtains permits and inspections

## 5. Pay smart

- Keep the deposit modest and pay as work is completed
- Never pay large sums in cash
- Hold the final payment until the work passes inspection and you've received lien waivers

## Red flags

- Door-to-door offers after a storm, especially with "leftover materials"
- Pressure to sign today
- Requests to pull permits in your name
- No physical business address
- A price that drops sharply when you hesitate

## Long Island specifics

- **Cesspools and septic**: Suffolk County runs a septic improvement grant program for qualifying homeowners who upgrade to nitrogen-reducing systems. Ask installers whether you qualify.
- **Heat pumps and solar**: state and utility incentives change often. Ask for a written breakdown of any rebates included in the price.
